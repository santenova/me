<Research/>

Short working notes from current AI and data research — what I tested, what held up, and what I abandoned.

2026-10-26[MEMORY]

Elasticsearch keeps the chat memory lane in one index

Conversation memory does not need a separate vector database. One Elasticsearch index holding a document per turn carries both the embedding and the original text, so a long-running chat retrieves its own past by keyword and by meaning in the same query — and stays filterable by conversation, persona and time.

  • One document per turn: conversation id, turn index and UTC timestamp as metadata
  • Dense vector field for recall, BM25 on the raw turn for exact names and ids
  • Recency decay applied to the score, never as a hard cutoff on the filter
  • Near-duplicate turns collapsed before they reach the prompt budget
  • Swapping the embedding model means re-embedding the whole lane, in place
2026-10-18[RETRIEVAL]

Hybrid retrieval keeps winning on small corpora

Pure vector search is convenient but leaks precision on internal collections of a few thousand documents. Blending keyword scoring with embeddings per query gives sharper top-10 results without a re-index.

  • Keyword weight tuned per collection, not globally
  • Re-ranker only pays off above a few hundred candidates
  • Latency stays flat when the blend runs in one pass
2026-10-04[FORENSICS]

What still gives a generated image away

Frequency-domain artefacts outlived every prompt-level tell I tested. Local noise statistics around high-contrast edges remain the most reliable single signal for separating generated imagery from camera output.

  • Compression history matters more than model family
  • Edge-region noise beats global histogram features
  • Batch pipelines must handle mixed compression first
2026-09-19[BENCHMARKING]

Cost and energy belong in the model scorecard

Accuracy tables are misleading when the cheapest model finishes the task. Every benchmark I run now records wall-clock time, token cost and energy per solved case alongside correctness.

  • Score per solved case, never per raw prompt
  • Small models often win once retries are counted
  • Keep the harness runnable on local hardware
2026-09-02[KNOWLEDGE BASES]

Append-only JSON stores age better than tidy schemas

Notes I have kept for years survive schema churn because writes only ever append. The reading layer adapts, the stored record stays immutable, and history remains queryable.

  • One event per line, timestamps in UTC
  • Migrate on read, never on write
  • Full-text index rebuilt from the append log

<Data Philosophy/>

I treat data as the durable asset and models as replaceable readers of it. The ethics below bank on open source and local deployment, so privacy, cost, jurisdiction and vendor decisions stay with the people who own the data.

01

Data Ethics

Banking on open source
01

Data Sovereignty

Complete data privacy with local deployment. No third parties reading prompts, because both the data and the models that read it stay on infrastructure you own.

02

Regulatory Compliance

Perfect for companies bound by GDPR, industry regulations, or government restrictions, where records and prompt content must never leave the company's own boundary.

02

AI Ethics

Banking on open and local solutions
03

Zero Operational AI Costs

Use open-source models like Ollama, reducing AI spend by 90%+. No per-token billing between you and the work.

04

Geopolitical Independence

No dependency on US tech giants or foreign governments. The stack keeps running regardless of vendor policy, export rules or regional outages.

05

Hybrid Architecture

Flexibility to mix local models with cloud APIs where appropriate: sensitive work stays in-house, everything else goes wherever it performs best.
